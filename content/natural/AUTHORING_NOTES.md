# Natural-language authoring notes

How to write the pools that `pnpm content:build` checks. The contract is docs/requirements.md
§13.4; this file collects what helps in practice.

## Files

- `en/` and `ja/` each hold `word.txt`, `line.txt`, and `paragraph.txt`. `ja/readings.txt` is
  generated by `pnpm content:build`; never edit it, and read the pairs a change adds in the diff.
- A word or sentence is one line. A paragraph is 2-10 consecutive lines, and a blank line starts
  the next one. Lines starting with `#` are comments.
- No spaces at either end of a line, and no tabs.

## English

- Words: 2-16 letters, no space. Sentences: 3-15 words, single spaces, letters, `'`, and `-`; no
  comma or period. Paragraphs: 2-10 lines of 2-15 words each, with at least one comma or period.
- No digits and no other marks. Write numbers as words.
- At most 88 columns a line.

## Japanese

- Follow every kanji with its reading in brackets: `今日[きょう]は晴[は]れです`. The reading
  belongs to the whole run of kanji before it, so `食[た]べる` reads only 食; write a compound whose
  reading does not split (`今日[きょう]`, `大人[おとな]`) as one.
- Use only the jōyō kanji. When a word needs another kanji, write it in kana.
- A reading is hiragana, katakana, or `ー`. Katakana words can be written in katakana as they are
  (`コーヒー`) and need no brackets.
- 、 and 。 belong to paragraphs only, and a paragraph has at least one.
- Not typable, and so refused: a lone small kana (write `きょう`, not `き` and `ょ` apart), ゐ, ゑ,
  ヴ, and a small tsu (っ) before ん, another っ, ー, or a mark.
- A line is at most 44 characters of text and 88 columns of romaji. Kana such as つ (`tsu`) and
  long ones such as `しゅ` (`shu`) make the romaji longer than the text, so a line that fits as
  text can still be too wide.
- Spell out what the reader must not guess: give the reading a word usually has, and avoid words
  whose reading depends on the sentence (行った is `い` or `おこな`; 上手 is `じょうず`). When in
  doubt, write the word in kana.

## Voice (Japanese)

- Write as Japanese is written and said, not as a translation: leave the subject out (no 私は, 彼は
  unless it is the point), use proverbs, set phrases, everyday remarks, notices, and letters.
- Words average about five characters: mix four-character idioms, katakana loanwords, greetings,
  and set phrases, and keep the mean near 5 when a change adds or removes words.
- Sentences are 短文 and paragraphs are 長文 in the interface; a sentence often has no 。 and a
  paragraph reads like a diary entry, a notice, or a short essay.
